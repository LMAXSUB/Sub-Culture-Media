Preliminary statement

It may be controversial to some, but we are here to tell you why Zola is the greatest entertainer that South Africa has produced.

We could tell you that Bonginkosi “Zola 7” Dlamini was born on 24 April 1977 and grew up in Zola, Soweto, which is where his stage name comes from. We could talk about SAMA awards, an Oscar and philanthropy. We will, but this is a portrait of why we believe Zola is the gold standard for the South African entertainment industry.

I

2004

In 2004 the SABC conducted a nationwide poll to determine the “100 Greatest South Africans” of all time. Nelson Mandela came first. Dr Christiaan Barnard, the cardiac surgeon who performed the world’s first human-to-human heart transplant, came second. Controversially, Mahatma Gandhi came third. The rest of the list, from fourth to seventeenth, included presidents like Thabo Mbeki, Nobel Prize winners, politicians, activists and even Shaka Zulu himself.

At number 22, the legend, then known as Zola 7, was placed only behind Brenda Fassie, who came in at number 18. But here’s the thing: Zola’s recognition did not come solely from being an excellent artist. It came because of who he was, and continues to be, to South Africa.

II

Prime Zola / Zola 7

Picture this: it is 1999, a short period after apartheid, and the Department of Education, in association with the South African Broadcasting Corporation, releases one of the greatest television series of all time.

Yizo Yizo, post-apartheid, was created to address a new struggle for young South Africans: a deteriorating schooling system. It assertively explored themes such as township life, youth culture, gangsterism, sexuality, school violence, drugs and the breakdown of the South African schooling system, in ways that the SABC has significantly fallen from since the heyday of shows like Yizo Yizo.

In those days, television was not merely entertainment; it was a mirror critiquing the very reality of life in contemporary South Africa. Each storyline was real and relatable, and characters like Gunman, Javas, Mantwa, Sticks and Tiza were as familiar as they were larger than life. It is during season 2 of the show that we meet Bonginkosi Dlamini, who would revolutionise the role of the legendary Papa Action, previously played by Ronnie Nyakale.

From a plethora of characters representing different figures in South African society, the most iconic are Chester and Papa Action, who personified gangsterism and how it contrasted with good standing in school, which metaphorically represented life.

Note on fileDumisani Dlamini is the real-life father of Doja Cat, born Amala Ratna Zandile Dlamini.

III

The soundtrack

Back then it was commonplace for a series to be accompanied by a score, feeding the African love of music. Each Yizo Yizo season had a soundtrack album released simultaneously, with songs featured throughout the series. This is where Zola came to be the greatest South African narrator. “Ghetto Fabulous”, featuring Kaybee, was an iconic song made after the success of “Ghetto Scandalous” from Zola’s debut album, Umdlwembe, released in 2000.

Umdlwembe, an album aimed at the youth of South Africa growing up in the ghettos, went platinum and was an early sign that Zola’s gift was not only creating art, but translating that art for a particular demographic: South Africa itself. “Umdlwembe” is the Zulu word for a problem child. Zola used his debut album to expose his feelings about the kind of neighbourhood he grew up in, vividly describing township life, the high crime rate and the violence required to survive such an environment. It is this kind of lore that made it apt for Zola to embody Papa Action on television.

Coming from the success of his music and acting careers, Zola was crowned the unofficial spokesperson for South Africa. Then known as “Zola 7”, he was gifted his own show by the SABC, on which he helped numerous individuals achieve their personal and social development goals. This endeared Zola to a society that already adored him, and certified the belief that Zola was not just a man, but a man of the people: the people’s champion.

IV

Tsotsi

In 2005 his country came calling again. He acted in the country’s biggest movie, Tsotsi, a story not far from the critique that Yizo Yizo made famous and which is ever prominent in Zola’s music. Once again, he did not only star in the Oscar-winning movie, he also led the soundtrack, which was the best choice for acquainting the world with what South Africa had become since the struggle days.

Judgment

Zola’s Portrait

Zola can look at his career through a lens and see a platinum-selling artist, or a very skilled actor. But his greatest attribute is being the country’s Voice.

Guilty as charged
