The account of accountants

An accountant is someone whose job is to keep, inspect and analyse financial records. Within the profession, a distinction exists between general and chartered accountants.

General accountants typically hold a university degree in accounting or finance. Chartered accountants must, in addition, complete professional exams, gain practical experience and maintain membership of a recognised professional body.1

The pay of chartered accountants

An entry-level chartered accountant averages R420 000 a year, which already sits above what many experienced general accountants earn. By mid-level that climbs to R720 000, and senior chartered accountants average R1 200 000. Above them is the top tier, where earnings reach R200 000 a month.

There is a catch. The money comes at the end of a long road, and the road itself pays little. Trainees in some 2026 listings were offered roughly R15 000 to R19 000 a month while studying and writing exams.

How to become a Chartered Accountant in South Africa

The CA(SA) route is run by the South African Institute of Chartered Accountants (SAICA). It takes about seven years in total, and every step is gated by study or exams.

  1. Finish matric with the right marks

    School

    You need a bachelor’s degree pass, with Maths and English both at level 5.

  2. Earn an accredited degree

    About 3 years

    Study a BCom in Accounting at a SAICA-accredited university. Not every accounting degree qualifies, so check the accreditation list before you apply.

  3. Complete the CTA

    About 1 year

    The Certificate in the Theory of Accounting is a postgraduate programme, sometimes offered as a postgraduate diploma. It qualifies you to write the first board exam.

  4. Sign a training contract

    3 years

    This is “articles”. You work at a SAICA-accredited training office, usually a Big Four firm (Deloitte, EY, KPMG or PwC), a mid-tier firm, or an approved company or public body. Studying part-time? The contract runs five years instead.

  5. Pass two board exams

    During training

    First comes the Initial Assessment of Competence, which was called the ITC until recently. After at least 20 months of training you can write the Assessment of Professional Competence (APC), the final exam.

  6. Register as a CA(SA)

    The finish line

    Once you have passed both exams and completed your contract, you apply to SAICA. You then keep your membership by meeting its continuing professional development requirements.

Not the only road

  • AGA(SA): SAICA’s Associate General Accountant designation gives professional recognition without the audit-focused training contract and board exams.
  • PA(SA): awarded by the South African Institute of Professional Accountants (SAIPA).
  • ACCA and CIMA: international designations, both active in South Africa.
  • Tax practitioners: SARS only registers tax practitioners who belong to an approved professional body, such as SAICA, SAIPA or SAIT.
  1. The two main accountancy bodies in South Africa are SAICA, which awards the CA(SA), and SAIPA, which awards the PA(SA).
